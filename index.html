    Community pharmacy · Philippines

    Establishment Dossier

    A sequenced checklist for opening a community pharmacy — licensing, layout, and compliance. Work the phases in order, and open any entry for the detail that step needs.

    PRC · FDA (CDRR) · DTI · BIR · LGU Requirements current as of 2025–2026
    00
    Before You Have a License
    Skip if already licensed
    Understand the pharmacist-in-charge (PIC) requirement
    One-time
    ▼

    Philippine law requires every pharmacy to have a licensed pharmacist physically present and in-charge during operating hours. Your options:

    • Be the PIC yourself — requires a valid PRC license as a pharmacist (RPh)
    • Hire a licensed RPh as PIC — they must be present during all operating hours. Part-time is possible but limited.
    • Split-shift PIC arrangement — two RPhs covering different shifts, both must be licensed and registered with FDA
    A pharmacy operating without a licensed pharmacist present is a major FDA violation and grounds for immediate LTO revocation. This is non-negotiable.
    Budget for PIC salary if hiring an RPh
    One-time
    ▼

    If you are not a licensed pharmacist, hiring a PIC is a fixed operating cost. Rough market rates in the Philippines (2025–2026):

    • Entry-level RPh (province / secondary city): ₱18,000–₱25,000/month
    • Entry-level RPh (Metro Manila / major cities): ₱22,000–₱32,000/month
    • Experienced RPh: ₱28,000–₱40,000/month+
    Factor this into your working capital from day one. Not having a PIC lined up before opening is a common mistake that delays launches.
    01
    Location Scouting
    Until a space is locked
    Scout your target area for viable commercial spaces
    DailyHigh priority
    ▼

    Walk or drive through your target area and observe:

    • Vacant commercial ground-floor spaces — note the address or drop a map pin
    • Demand generators nearby: clinics, barangay health centers, hospitals, lying-in centers, dental offices
    • Existing pharmacies — how busy do they look? Are they franchise or independent?
    • Foot traffic quality: are people running errands, or just passing through?
    • Public transport access: jeepney routes, tricycle terminals, or main roads
    The best pharmacy locations are within 50–200m of a clinic cluster, hospital outpatient gate, or barangay health center. Prescriptions = guaranteed foot traffic.
    Check Facebook Marketplace and OLX for commercial spaces for rent
    Daily
    ▼

    Search: "commercial space for rent [your city]" on Facebook Marketplace and OLX.ph.

    • Ground floor only — upper floors are not allowed for pharmacies
    • Minimum 20 sqm — smaller spaces will fail FDA floor area requirements
    • Screenshot or save promising listings immediately — they disappear fast
    • Message 1–2 landlords even if you're not ready — ask for floor plan and monthly rate
    • Also check your city's local Facebook Buy & Sell groups
    Log every space you find in a running tracker
    Daily
    ▼

    Use a phone note, Google Sheet, or spreadsheet. One row per space with these columns:

    • Address / landmark — specific enough to find it again
    • Size estimate — eyeballed sqm or stated in listing
    • Monthly rent — if known
    • Nearest demand generator — clinic name, hospital, health center
    • Nearest competitor pharmacy — name + estimated distance
    • Status — Spotted / Inquired / Visited / Rejected / Shortlisted
    Update this same day. Memory is unreliable when juggling multiple spaces — the tracker is your external brain.
    Verify zoning for any shortlisted space
    One-time per space
    ▼

    Before committing to a space, confirm it's zoned for commercial use:

    • Ask the landlord directly — "Is this space zoned for commercial/retail?" Most know the answer.
    • City Hall — Zoning / Urban Planning Office — Bring the address, they can confirm in one visit. No appointment usually needed.
    A pharmacy in a residentially-zoned space will fail the LGU Mayor's Permit step. Verify this early — it can void months of preparation.
    Physically visit and assess your top 1–2 spaces
    One-time per space
    ▼

    When you visit in person, check:

    • Actual floor area — use a tape measure or phone AR measure tool
    • Layout: room for dispensing counter, shelving, consultation nook, and a refrigerator?
    • Electrical: at least 3 usable outlets, no obvious hazards — FDA will inspect this
    • Ventilation and natural or artificial lighting — also FDA-inspected
    • Signage visibility — can a passerby read your sign from the road or sidewalk?
    • Lease terms: monthly vs annual, advance + deposit amount, landlord's contact
    • Any existing fit-out you can repurpose (shelving, counters, AC)
    Minimum space is ~20 sqm per FDA guidelines. 25–35 sqm is more practical for compliant layout + future growth.
    02
    Regulatory Requirements
    Run parallel to Phase 1
    Download and read the FDA LTO checklist for drugstores
    One-time
    ▼

    Go to fda.gov.ph → Center for Drug Regulation and Research (CDRR) → Licensing → Drugstore LTO.

    • Download the current checklist — requirements change, always get the live version
    • Read fully once. Don't act yet — just understand what's coming.
    • Key things to note: space requirements, equipment list, pharmacist documentation
    • Save the PDF in a dedicated "Pharmacy Permits" folder
    The FDA calls it a "drugstore" LTO, not "pharmacy." Use that term when searching. Also note: LTO has initial and annual renewal requirements — understand both.
    Register your business name with DTI
    One-time
    ▼

    For sole proprietorship, register at bnrs.dti.gov.ph (can be done fully online).

    • Check name availability first
    • Choose Regional scope to cover your province and nearby areas (₱500–2,000 fee)
    • Download and print the Certificate of Business Name Registration — required for LGU permits

    For partnerships or corporations, register with the SEC instead. This adds complexity and cost but may be worth it for larger capital setups or multiple owners.

    If going franchise, confirm whether the franchisor handles the business name registration or if you need to do it independently.
    Compile your master documents folder
    One-time
    ▼

    Every permit application will ask for overlapping documents. Prepare physical and digital copies of:

    • DTI Certificate of Business Name Registration
    • PRC License of the pharmacist-in-charge (yours or your hired RPh)
    • PRC Registration — check renewal date, it must be current
    • Valid government IDs — at least 2 types
    • NBI Clearance — get a fresh one (many applications require recent issuance)
    • Passport-size photos — bring 6+ copies (different agencies ask for different sizes)
    • Proof of business address: lease contract or landlord's authorization letter
    Scan everything into a Google Drive folder labeled "Pharmacy Permits — Master Docs." You will submit these repeatedly to different agencies. Digital backups save repeat trips.
    Secure LGU permits (once location is confirmed)
    One-time
    ▼

    LGU permits are required before you can open. Process these in order:

    • Barangay Clearance — from the barangay where your pharmacy is located. Usually the first step.
    • Zoning Clearance — confirms commercial use. Bring lease contract and floor plan.
    • Sanitary Permit — from the City/Municipal Health Office. They inspect your space.
    • Fire Safety Inspection Certificate — Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP). They inspect exits, extinguishers, and fire safety compliance.
    • Mayor's Permit / Business Permit — from the City / Municipal Hall. This requires all the above to be completed first.
    Process these in this order — each one feeds the next. Going out of order causes delays. Budget ₱5,000–₱20,000 total for LGU permit fees depending on your city.
    Register with BIR
    One-time
    ▼

    Register your pharmacy business with the Bureau of Internal Revenue at your Revenue District Office (RDO):

    • Register as a business taxpayer (BIR Form 1901 for sole proprietors)
    • Get a Certificate of Registration (COR)
    • Have your official receipts and sales invoices printed by a BIR-accredited printer
    • Register your books of account (journal, ledger, etc.)
    • Know your tax type: likely Percentage Tax (3%) if projected annual gross sales are under the VAT threshold (₱3M as of 2025)
    You cannot legally issue official receipts without BIR registration. Pharmacies are audited. Get this right from day one.
    Apply for FDA License to Operate (LTO)
    One-timeCritical gate
    ▼

    The FDA LTO is the final gate before you can legally operate as a pharmacy. Apply at fda.gov.ph or at the FDA regional office.

    • Submit all required documents per the checklist you downloaded earlier
    • Expect a physical inspection of your pharmacy space by an FDA inspector
    • They will check: layout, equipment, storage conditions, temperature logs, drug arrangement, labeling, signage
    • Processing time: typically 1–3 months. Follow up regularly.
    • Pay the LTO fee (varies by category and drug types carried)
    Do NOT open to the public before your LTO is issued. Operating without an LTO is a criminal violation under RA 9711. Wait for the physical certificate.
    03
    Franchise vs. Independent
    Decide before capital planning
    Request franchise information packets from TGP and Generika
    One-time
    ▼

    Even if you're leaning independent, franchise packets are a free, detailed compliance roadmap.

    • TGP (The Generics Pharmacy) — tgp.com.ph → Franchising → fill inquiry form
    • Generika Drugstore — generika.com.ph → Franchise → fill inquiry form
    • MedExpress — also worth checking for comparison

    What to compare in each packet: total investment required, franchise fee, monthly royalty/service fee, territory exclusivity, supplier access, and what they handle for you (permits, layout, training).

    TGP is generally the lowest-barrier PH pharmacy franchise (~₱300–500k all-in). Generika is more established. Use their checklists as a compliance template even if you go independent.
    Make your franchise vs. independent decision
    One-time
    ▼

    Key factors to weigh:

    • Capital: Franchise requires more upfront (franchise fee + setup standards). Independent is leaner.
    • Brand recognition: Franchise gives you instant trust. Independent must earn it.
    • Supplier access: Franchises have pre-negotiated wholesale deals. Independent suppliers require your own relationships.
    • Autonomy: Independent gives full control over products, pricing, layout. Franchise is constrained by the franchisor.
    • Support: Franchises guide you through setup, training, and compliance. Independent means figuring it out yourself.
    There's no universally right answer. First-timers with limited pharmacy business experience often benefit from franchise support during setup. Experienced operators or those with strong supplier networks tend to prefer independent.
    04
    Capital Planning
    Before signing any lease
    Build a full capital estimate spreadsheet
    One-time
    ▼

    Create a spreadsheet with realistic estimates for every cost category:

    • Renovation / fit-out — ₱150,000–₱400,000 depending on space condition
    • Initial drug inventory — ₱300,000–₱700,000 (generics-focused is cheaper to start)
    • Equipment: shelving, refrigerator (for thermolabile drugs), dispensing counter, POS system, CCTV — ₱80,000–₱180,000
    • Signage and branding — ₱20,000–₱60,000
    • Permits and LTO fees — ₱20,000–₱60,000
    • Lease advance + deposit — typically 2–3 months rent upfront
    • Working capital — 3 months of: rent + utilities + PIC salary (if applicable) + misc — ₱150,000–₱300,000
    • Franchise fee — ₱0 (if independent) to ₱200,000+ (if franchise)
    Total rough range: ₱700,000–₱1,700,000 depending on city, space, and setup type. Metro Manila will be at the higher end. Provincial and secondary cities will be lower.
    Identify and confirm your funding source
    One-timeDon't delay
    ▼

    Common funding paths for first-time pharmacy owners in the Philippines:

    • Personal savings — cleanest. No repayment pressure during early operations.
    • Family loan or investment — common and viable. Clarify upfront: loan vs. gift vs. ownership stake. Get it in writing even if informal.
    • Bank loan / SBA loan — requires collateral, business plan, and credit history. Slower to process but larger amounts possible.
    • Pag-IBIG / SSS loan — lower amounts, longer processing, but accessible if you're a member.
    • DOST / DTI MSME grants — check current programs; availability varies by year and region.
    Whatever the source — have the money confirmed and accessible before signing a lease. Do not sign a lease hoping the loan will come through. The landlord won't wait.
    Source drug suppliers and get pricing lists
    One-time
    ▼

    Your inventory cost depends heavily on supplier relationships. Start reaching out before you open:

    • Zuellig Pharma — largest pharmaceutical distributor in PH. Requires accreditation.
    • Medilink / Cathay Drug — alternative distributors with different product mixes
    • Direct manufacturer reps — some brands offer better margins direct vs. through distributors
    • Ask about: minimum order quantities, credit terms (COD vs. 15/30 days), return policies, cold chain for thermolabile drugs
    For a generics-focused startup pharmacy, look at PITC Pharma (government distributor of generics) for competitive pricing on essential medicines.
    05
    Physical Setup
    After lease is signed
    Design and build out your pharmacy layout
    One-time
    ▼

    FDA-compliant pharmacy layout must include:

    • Dispensing area — clearly marked, with a counter separating staff from customers
    • Drug storage area — shelving organized by drug category, away from direct sunlight
    • Refrigerator — for thermolabile drugs (insulin, some vaccines, eye drops). Dedicated pharmaceutical refrigerator required.
    • Consultation area — even a small designated space satisfies GPP requirements
    • Prescription file / records storage — for maintaining prescription logs (required by law)
    • Signage — pharmacy name, PIC name with PRC license number, operating hours
    Get a simple floor plan drawn (even hand-sketched) before fit-out. You'll need to submit it to the FDA with your LTO application.
    Stock your initial inventory
    One-time
    ▼

    For a first pharmacy, start lean and focused rather than trying to carry everything:

    • Essential generic medicines (paracetamol, amoxicillin, mefenamic acid, metformin, amlodipine, etc.)
    • OTC bestsellers: vitamins, antacids, antihistamines, topical antiseptics
    • High-turnover maintenance meds for your area's prevalent conditions
    • Basic medical supplies: syringes, alcohol, cotton, bandages
    • Do NOT over-invest in slow-moving SKUs at launch — let demand tell you what to stock
    The Generics Act (RA 6675) requires pharmacies to carry generic equivalents of branded drugs. Compliance is mandatory and inspected.
    Set up your POS, records system, and temperature logs
    One-time
    ▼
    • POS system — even a simple one (PharmaPOS, or a tablet-based solution). Tracks sales, inventory, and issues BIR-compliant receipts.
    • Prescription logbook — required by law. Log all dispensed prescription drugs with: date, patient name, prescriber, drug, quantity.
    • Temperature monitoring log — daily min/max temperature record for your storage area and refrigerator. FDA inspectors check this.
    • Drug expiry tracking — system or manual log for first-expiry-first-out (FEFO) inventory management
    Regulated drugs (e.g., controlled substances, antibiotics) have stricter record-keeping rules under PDEA and DOH. Review RA 9165 requirements if you plan to carry these.
    ✦
    Weekly Check-ins
    Every 7 days
    Review your location tracker and update statuses
    Weekly
    ▼

    Go through every space you've logged and ask:

    • Have any "Spotted" been upgraded to "Inquired" yet? If not — why not?
    • Any previously available spaces now taken? Remove or flag them.
    • Do you have at least 3 shortlisted candidates? If not — scout more this week.
    • Which space is currently looking most promising, and what's blocking you from moving forward on it?
    Check for new FDA or LGU regulatory updates
    Weekly
    ▼

    Takes 5 minutes. Requirements change without much notice:

    • Check fda.gov.ph for new circulars or advisories under drugstores / pharmaceutical establishments
    • Check your city / municipal hall announcements for business permit updates
    • Join Facebook groups: "Pharmacy Owners Philippines" or "RPh Philippines" — practitioners often post real-world regulatory updates faster than official channels
    Follow up on any pending permit applications
    Weekly
    ▼

    Government processing moves slowly unless you follow up. Every week:

    • Call or visit any agency with a pending application — FDA, LGU, BIR, DTI
    • Ask for a specific timeline or next step, not just "it's being processed"
    • Log the date, who you spoke to, and what they said — paper trails matter if something gets lost
    • If an agency asks for an additional document, provide it within 24–48 hours to avoid queue delays
    The FDA LTO is the slowest step. Applicants who follow up weekly get processed faster than those who wait passively.
